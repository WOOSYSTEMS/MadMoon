## 1. Health Canada Cosmetic Notification (CNF)

### What It Is

The Cosmetic Notification Form (CNF) is a mandatory notification (NOT an approval) that you must submit to Health Canada. It does not mean Health Canada approves or endorses your product -- it is a registry for tracking purposes and safety oversight.

### Legal Basis

- Food and Drugs Act, Section 30
- Cosmetic Regulations (C.R.C., c. 869)

### Timeline

You must submit the CNF **within 10 days of your first sale** in Canada. "First sale" = the first time the product is available for purchase by any Canadian consumer, whether at a farmers market, online, or to a coworker.

### Cost

**FREE.** There is no fee to file a CNF.

### How to Submit

1. Create a GCKey or Sign-In Canada account
2. Access the Health Canada Cosmetic Notification System (CNS) portal
3. Fill out the CNF online
4. Submit electronically (paper submissions are not accepted)

### Key Links

- Official guide: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/cosmetics/notification-cosmetics/guide.html
- Common submission issues: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/cosmetics/notification-cosmetics/common-issues-submitting.html
- How Health Canada regulates cosmetics: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/cosmetics/how-we-regulate-cosmetics.html
- Apply for business license (Kamloops portal, for reference): https://www.kamloops.ca/eApply

### Information Required on the CNF

| Field | What to Enter for BadMoon |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Product name | BadMoon Solid Cologne Balm |
| Product category | Leave-on (cologne/fragrance) |
| Product type | Fragrance / Perfume |
| Intended use | Solid fragrance for skin application |
| Target population | Adults |
| Full INCI ingredient list | Petrolatum, Cedrus atlantica Bark Oil (or Juniperus virginiana Oil), Mentha viridis Leaf Oil |
| Concentration ranges | Must provide percentage range for each ingredient |
| Fragrance allergens | Must individually declare any of the 24 List 1 allergens if present above 0.001% (leave-on threshold) |
| Manufacturer name | Your legal business name (e.g., "Noor Digital" or "BadMoon") |
| Canadian address | Your Kamloops address |
| Contact person | Your name, title, phone, email |

### Fragrance Allergen Disclosure (NEW as of April 12, 2026)

Starting April 12, 2026, you must individually declare fragrance allergens on both the CNF and the product label when present above:
- **0.001% in leave-on products** (your solid cologne IS a leave-on product)
- 0.01% in rinse-off products

**Check your cedarwood and spearmint essential oil supplier's Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the presence of any of the 24 List 1 allergens.** Common allergens found in cedarwood and spearmint oils may include limonene, linalool, and geraniol depending on the source plant and distillation. Your supplier should provide this data.

From August 1, 2026, List 2 allergens (expanded to 81 substances total) must also be declared for new products.

## 2. Labelling Requirements

### Legal Basis

- Cosmetic Regulations (C.R.C., c. 869), Sections 17-21
- Consumer Packaging and Labelling Act (CPLA)
- Consumer Packaging and Labelling Regulations
- Official guide: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/labelling-cosmetics.html

### What Must Appear on the Label

| Element | Requirement | Language |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| Product identity | "Solid Cologne Balm" or "Baume de cologne solide" | **Bilingual (EN + FR)** |
| Net quantity | In metric (e.g., "15 g / 0.5 oz") | **Bilingual (EN + FR)** |
| Ingredient list | Headed "Ingredients:" | **INCI names only (no translation needed)** |
| Manufacturer name | Your business name | Either language |
| Manufacturer address | Your Kamloops address | Either language |
| Warnings/cautions | If applicable (e.g., "For external use only") | **Bilingual (EN + FR)** |

### INCI Ingredient Names for BadMoon

List ingredients in **descending order of predominance** (by weight):

| Common Name | INCI Name |
|-------------|-----------|
| Petroleum jelly (Vaseline) | **Petrolatum** |
| Cedarwood essential oil (Atlas) | **Cedrus atlantica Bark Oil** |
| Cedarwood essential oil (Virginia) | **Juniperus virginiana Oil** |
| Spearmint essential oil | **Mentha viridis Leaf Oil** |

Use the INCI name that matches your specific cedarwood source. Check the bottle or supplier documentation.

### Font Size Requirements for Net Quantity

The numerical portion of the net quantity must be in **boldface type** with minimum height based on principal display surface area:

| Principal Display Surface | Minimum Font Height |
|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Up to 32 cm2 (5 in2) | 1.6 mm (1/16 in) |
| 32-258 cm2 (5-40 in2) | 3.2 mm (1/8 in) |
| 258-645 cm2 (40-100 in2) | 6.4 mm (1/4 in) |

For a small tin (likely under 32 cm2), the minimum is 1.6 mm -- but make it larger for readability.

### Example Label Text

**Outer label / tin lid or wrap:**

```
BADMOON
Solid Cologne Balm / Baume de cologne solide
15 g / 0.5 oz

Ingredients: Petrolatum, Cedrus atlantica Bark Oil,
Mentha viridis Leaf Oil

[Your Business Name]
[Street Address]
Kamloops, BC V2C [postal code]
Canada

For external use only. / Usage externe seulement.
Avoid contact with eyes. / Eviter le contact avec les yeux.
```

**Notes:**
- INCI names do NOT need French translation
- "Ingredients:" heading does NOT need French translation (internationally recognized term)
- Product identity, net quantity, and warnings MUST be bilingual
- If the container is too small for all info, you can use a tag, tape, or outer packaging

## 3. Product Safety

### Concentration Limits for Essential Oils

#### Cedarwood Essential Oil

| Source | Maximum Dermal Limit |
|--------|---------------------|
| Tisserand & Young (Essential Oil Safety, 2nd Ed.) | ~4-5% maximum |
| IFRA (leave-on products) | Varies by type; generally permitted at higher levels |
| Recommended for leave-on balm | **2-3% maximum** (conservative, safe) |

Cedarwood oil can be a skin sensitizer. Virginia cedarwood (Juniperus virginiana) has been flagged as irritating to skin.

#### Spearmint Essential Oil

| Source | Maximum Dermal Limit |
|--------|---------------------|
| Tisserand & Young | **1.7% maximum** |
| IFRA Category 4 (body products) | ~0.45% |
| Recommended for leave-on balm | **1.5% maximum** (conservative, safe) |

Spearmint oil is a potential mucous membrane irritant and a mild skin sensitizer.

#### Recommended Safe Formula for BadMoon

| Ingredient | Percentage |
|------------|-----------|
| Petrolatum | 95-97% |
| Cedarwood essential oil | 1.5-2.5% |
| Spearmint essential oil | 0.5-1.5% |
| **Total essential oils** | **2-4% combined** |

Stay under 1.7% for spearmint specifically. Total fragrance load of 2-3% is a safe, conservative target for a leave-on product.

### Health Canada Cosmetic Ingredient Hotlist

The Hotlist is Health Canada's list of prohibited and restricted cosmetic ingredients.

- **Petrolatum:** NOT on the Hotlist. Permitted.
- **Cedarwood oil:** NOT on the Hotlist. Permitted.
- **Spearmint oil:** NOT on the Hotlist. Permitted.

None of your three ingredients are banned or restricted. You are clear.

Official Hotlist: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredient-hotlist.html

### Allergen Declaration

Under the new April 2026 rules, you must check your essential oil suppliers' documentation for the presence of any of the 24 regulated fragrance allergens. Common ones that may appear in cedarwood and spearmint oils:

- Limonene
- Linalool
- Geraniol
- Citronellol
- Eugenol

If any are present above **0.001% in the finished product** (leave-on threshold), they must be:
1. Listed individually on the product label (in the ingredient list)
2. Declared on the CNF

**Action item:** Request your essential oil supplier's allergen breakdown / IFRA certificate / GC-MS analysis report.

## 5. Tax Requirements

### GST/HST

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Small supplier threshold | **$30,000** in worldwide taxable revenue |
| Measured over | 4 consecutive calendar quarters, or any single quarter |
| Below $30K | You are NOT required to register or charge GST/HST |
| Above $30K | Must register within 29 days of crossing the threshold |
| GST rate in BC | **5% GST** (BC does not have HST; it uses GST + PST) |
| PST on cosmetics in BC | **7% PST** applies to most tangible goods including cosmetics |
| Registration | CRA Business Number: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/registering-your-business.html |

### Do Cosmetics Have Additional Tax?

No special additional tax. Standard GST (5%) + BC PST (7%) = 12% combined for tangible personal property sold in BC.

### Record Keeping

- Keep all receipts for ingredients, packaging, labels, equipment
- Track all sales (date, amount, customer if applicable)
- Keep records for **6 years** minimum (CRA requirement)
- Track mileage if driving to markets
- Separate business bank account recommended (not legally required for sole prop but strongly advised)

**Is it legally required?** No, not by law. But it is:

- Strongly recommended for any product applied to skin

## 9. Cosmetic vs. Drug: What You Can and Cannot Say

### The Legal Line

In Canada, if your product claims to treat, prevent, or cure a disease or modify a body function, it is classified as a **drug** (or Natural Health Product), NOT a cosmetic. The classification is based primarily on the **claims you make**, not just the ingredients.

### Legal Basis

- Food and Drugs Act, Sections 2 and 3
- Natural Health Products Regulations

### Safe Claims (Cosmetic -- OK to Say)

These describe appearance, feel, scent, or cleansing:

- "Smells great"
- "Long-lasting fragrance"
- "Moisturizes skin"
- "Softens skin"
- "Conditions skin"
- "Leaves skin feeling smooth"
- "Fresh cedar and mint scent"
- "Apply to pulse points for all-day fragrance"
- "Handmade with natural ingredients"
- "Travel-friendly solid cologne"

### Dangerous Claims (Drug Territory -- DO NOT Say)

These imply therapeutic or physiological effects:

| DO NOT Say | Why |
|------------|-----|
| "Heals dry skin" | Therapeutic claim |
| "Treats eczema/acne/rash" | Disease treatment |
| "Anti-inflammatory" | Drug claim |
| "Reduces stress/anxiety" | Therapeutic claim |
| "Boosts circulation" | Body function modification |
| "Antibacterial" / "Antimicrobial" | Drug claim |
| "Pain relief" | Drug claim |
| "Promotes healing" | Drug claim |
| "Restores skin" | Implies therapeutic repair |
| "Prevents aging" | Drug claim (vs. "reduces appearance of fine lines") |
| "Detoxifies" | Physiological claim |
| "Stimulates hair growth" | Drug claim |
| "Calming" (in a physiological context) | Can cross into NHP territory |

### Words to Avoid Entirely

**restores, repairs, prevents, stimulates, eliminates, stops, heals, treats, cures, therapeutic, medicinal, anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, detox**

### Safe Marketing Angle for BadMoon

Focus on:
- Scent experience ("bold cedar and cool mint")
- Convenience ("solid format, no spills, TSA-friendly")
- Craftsmanship ("handmade in small batches in Kamloops, BC")
- Application ("dab on wrists, neck, and chest")
- Vibe/identity ("for the man who moves different")

## Notes

- **Your 3 ingredients (petrolatum, cedarwood oil, spearmint oil) are all permitted by Health Canada.** None appear on the Hotlist as banned or restricted.
- **Solid cologne is classified as a cosmetic** in Canada as long as you only make cosmetic claims (scent, feel, appearance). The moment you claim it heals, treats, or modifies body function, it becomes a drug.
- **The CNF is free and must be filed within 10 days of first sale.** This is the single most important regulatory step.
- **Bilingual labelling is mandatory** for product identity, net quantity, and warnings. INCI ingredient names are universal and do not need translation.
- **Keep essential oil concentrations conservative** -- 2-3% total is safe and effective for a solid cologne. Spearmint must stay under 1.7%.
- **Insurance is not legally required but practically essential** -- farmers markets and retailers will demand it.
