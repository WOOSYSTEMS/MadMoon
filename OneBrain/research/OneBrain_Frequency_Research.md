# 1. PHYSICS OF BEHAVIORAL FREQUENCIES

## 1.1 Frequency Ranges and Biological Effects

### Infrasound (0.1-20 Hz)

Infrasound travels long distances with minimal attenuation and permeates biological tissue due to its large particle displacement and deep penetration.

**Key Studies:**

- **Stress physiology (2026):** A randomized controlled study at MacEwan University (Alberta, Canada) exposed 36 adults to 18 Hz infrasound via hidden subwoofers. Infrasound was associated with elevated salivary cortisol and higher self-reported irritability, disinterest, and sadness -- even though participants could not reliably detect whether infrasound was present.
  - *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience*, 2026. [Full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/behavioral-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnbeh.2026.1729876/full)

- **Brain connectivity changes (fMRI):** Weichenberger, Bauer, Kuhler et al. (2017) used fMRI and found that near-threshold infrasound (12 Hz) produced significantly higher local connectivity in the right superior temporal gyrus, the anterior cingulate cortex, and the right amygdala.
  - Weichenberger M et al. "Altered cortical and subcortical connectivity due to infrasound administered near the hearing threshold." *PLoS ONE* 12(4): e0174420, 2017. [Full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0174420)

- **The "fear frequency" at ~18.9 Hz:** Vic Tandy discovered a defective extractor fan producing infrasound at 18.98 Hz -- close to the resonant frequency of the human eyeball -- caused feelings of anxiety, peripheral visual disturbances, and chills.
  - Tandy V & Lawrence TR. "The ghost in the machine." *Journal of the Society for Psychical Research* 62(851): 360-364, 1998.

- **Long-term exposure (28 nights, null behavioral findings):** Ascone et al. exposed 38 participants to 6 Hz infrasound at 80-90 dB for 28 consecutive nights. Null findings for all behavioral variables, but voxel-based morphometry revealed gray matter decreases in cerebellum VIIIa and left angular gyrus.
  - Ascone L et al. *Scientific Reports* 11, 2791, 2021. [Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-82203-6)

### Audible Subliminal Frequencies (20 Hz - 20 kHz at sub-threshold volumes) [MACBOOK]

- **Subconscious hearing route (2026):** Jurado & Marquardt demonstrated that infrasound and very low-frequency tones produce intracochlear electrical potentials via displacement-coupled outer hair cells (OHCs), even without mechanical activation of inner hair cells (IHCs). This represents a "subconscious hearing route" where sound exerts effects below the hearing threshold.
  - Jurado C & Marquardt T. "Infrasound sensation is mediated by intracochlear electrical potentials." *Scientific Reports*, 2026. [Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-50179-w)

- **Low-frequency inner ear micromechanics:** Kugler et al. (2014) showed sensory cells in the human inner ear react to pressure waves below 100 Hz, producing detectable micromechanical responses even at near-threshold levels.
  - Kugler K et al. *Royal Society Open Science* 1(2): 140166, 2014. [Full text](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/1/2/140166/36427/Low-frequency-sound-affects-active-micromechanics)

- **Binaural beats and frequency following response:** When two tones of slightly different frequencies are presented to each ear, the brainstem's superior olivary nucleus generates a perceived "beat" at the difference frequency. Evidence for reliable brainwave entrainment remains mixed.
  - Gao X et al. *International Journal of Psychophysiology* 94(3): 399-406, 2014.
  - Orozco Perez HD et al. *Scientific Reports*, 2025. [Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-88517-z)

### Ultrasound (>20 kHz)

- **Sub-threshold ultrasound alters cognition:** Weichenberger et al. (2022) found that a 21.5 kHz tone at 10 dB BELOW hearing threshold produced functional changes in the cognitive control network (bilateral inferior frontal gyrus). Effects appeared ONLY when ultrasound was below threshold, not above it.
  - Weichenberger M et al. *PLoS ONE* 17(12): e0277727, 2022. [Full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0277727)

- **Transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS):** [HARDWARE] Ultrasound can directly modulate neural activity by opening mechanosensitive ion channels (Piezo1, Piezo2, TRPC1) in neural tissue. Confirmed as a mechanical (not thermal) mechanism.
  - Tyler WJ et al. *Nature Communications* 13, 493, 2022. [Full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28040-1)
  - Yu K, Niu X, He B. *IEEE Transactions on Biomedical Engineering*, 2024. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748529/)

### ELF Electromagnetic (3-30 Hz) [HARDWARE]

- **Neural differentiation:** ELF-EMF promotes neuronal differentiation via TRPC1 up-regulation.
  - Ma Q et al. *PLoS ONE* 11(3): e0150923, 2016. [Full text](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0150923)

- **Dose-dependent neuroplasticity:** Low-intensity ELF-EMF (1 mT) increased pro-neuroplastic factors; high-intensity (7 mT) decreased them -- inverted-U dose-response.
  - *PubMed*, 2025. PMID: 41544927.

- **Persinger's temporal lobe stimulation:** Weak (~1 microTesla), patterned transcerebral magnetic fields induced "sensed presences" and mystical-type experiences, though replication has been inconsistent.
  - Persinger MA. *Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease* 190(8): 533-541, 2002.

### VLF Electromagnetic (3-30 kHz) [HARDWARE]

- Natural VLF atmospherics (sferics from lightning) provoke increases in alpha EEG power and beta power in the right hemisphere. Associated with "weather sensitivity."
  - Schienle A et al. *Journal of Scientific Exploration* 15(1): 33-49, 2001.

## 1.3 Sound Waves Interacting with Neural Tissue Without Conscious Perception

### Cochlear Processing Below Awareness [MACBOOK-relevant]

- **OHC-mediated intracochlear electrical potentials (2026):** Outer hair cells generate electrical potentials that act on inner hair cell membranes and cause synaptic release leading to auditory sensation -- even at infrasonic frequencies where normal IHC mechanical activation does not occur. This is a genuine "subconscious hearing route."
  - Jurado C & Marquardt T (2026), cited above.

- **Modulation of cochlear amplifier:** Low-frequency tones modulate distortion product otoacoustic emissions at near-threshold levels.
  - Drexl M et al. *JARO* 23: 43-59, 2022.

### Bone Conduction Pathways

Five distinct pathways to the cochlea:
1. Osseotympanic pathway (sound pressure in ear canal)
2. Inertial forces on middle ear ossicles
3. Inertial forces on cochlear fluid directly
4. Compression of cochlear space
5. Sound pressure from skull interior via cerebrospinal fluid (Sohmer pathway)

All five ultimately produce the same basilar membrane vibration as air conduction.
- Stenfelt S & Goode RL. *Hearing Research*, 2013.

### Key Finding: Sub-threshold vs. Supra-threshold

The Weichenberger et al. (2022) study is critical: air-conducted ultrasound at 21.5 kHz produced cognitive control network changes ONLY when presented BELOW the hearing threshold, not above it. Sub-threshold frequencies may engage different neural processing pathways because attentional suppression mechanisms are not activated for imperceptible stimuli.

## 1.4 Air-Conducted vs. Bone-Conducted Subliminal Influence

| Feature | Air Conduction | Bone Conduction |
|---|---|---|
| Pathway | Outer ear -> middle ear -> cochlea | Skull vibration -> cochlea (5 pathways) |
| Cochlear endpoint | Same basilar membrane transduction | Same basilar membrane transduction |
| Middle ear bypass | No | 3 of 5 pathways bypass it |
| Masking by ambient noise | High susceptibility | Lower susceptibility |
| Frequency sensitivity | Full audible range | Better low-frequency transmission |

**Soft tissue conduction** has been confirmed: participants perceived and repeated words via non-osseous tissue pathways.
- *PMC*, 2024. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10886245/)

**Cochlear-bone wave:** A slow wave propagating along cochlear bone at ~90 m/s produces both hearing sensation and otoacoustic emissions.
- Sisto R et al. *Nature Communications* 5, 4160, 2014.

# 2. ANIMAL BEHAVIORAL CONTROL

## 2.1 Marine Mammals

### Harbor Porpoise Pingers
- Kraus SD et al. (1997). "Acoustic alarms reduce porpoise mortality." *Nature*, 388, 525.
  - Frequency: Broadband, peaks at 3 kHz and 20 kHz; 121.7-124.7 dB
  - Results: 47 porpoises caught in control nets vs. only 2 in alarmed nets (p = 0.001)
  - [Source](https://www.nature.com/articles/41451)

- Habituation occurs within 10-11 days (Kastelein et al.)

### "Dinner Bell" Effect
Pinnipeds habituate and may learn to associate pinger sounds with fish, turning deterrents into attractants.
- Schakner ZA & Blumstein DT. *Biological Conservation*.

### Beaked Whale Strandings
- DeRuiter SL et al. (2013). Mid-frequency active sonar (3-8 kHz) caused Cuvier's beaked whales to cease echolocation and swim rapidly away at received levels as low as 89-127 dB. Multiple mass strandings associated with naval sonar.
  - [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3730631/)

## 2.2 Insects

### Moth-Bat Arms Race
- Roeder KD (1967). Two-neuron auditory system: A1 cell evokes controlled flight away from distant bats; A2 activates erratic evasion at close range.
- Corcoran AJ et al. (2009). Tiger moth jams bat sonar with dense ultrasonic clicks. *Science*, 325(5938), 325-327. [Source](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.1174096)

### Cricket Phonotaxis
- Moiseff A et al. (1978). *PNAS*, 75(8), 4052-4056.
  - Positive phonotaxis (attraction): 5 kHz
  - Negative phonotaxis (avoidance): 12-100 kHz at low intensity

  - Response latency: 40-80 ms

### Mosquitoes [MACBOOK - frequency generation possible]

  - Cator LM et al. (2009). *Science*, 323(5917), 1077-1079.

# 3. HUMAN BEHAVIORAL INFLUENCE

## 3.1 Vic Tandy's Infrasound Research

**Original Paper:** Tandy V & Lawrence TR (1998). "The Ghost in the Machine." *JSPR*, 62(851), 360-364.

Tandy was an experimental officer at Coventry University. While working late in a medical lab, he experienced intense anxiety and a grey blob in peripheral vision. A defective extractor fan was producing a standing wave at **18.98 Hz** -- the half-wavelength fit exactly across the lab's length.

**Follow-up:** Tandy (2000) investigated medieval cellars beneath Coventry's Tourist Information Centre and found 19 Hz standing waves at the exact location visitors reported apparitional experiences.

**Replication Status:** No formal large-scale replication published. The controlled "Haunt Project" (French et al., 2009, N=79) FAILED to replicate the infrasound-ghost link.

## 3.4 Binaural Beats [MACBOOK]

### Mechanism
Two tones of slightly different frequencies to each ear via headphones. Brain perceives "phantom" beat at difference frequency. E.g., 400 Hz left + 410 Hz right = 10 Hz alpha beat.

### Meta-Analyses

**Garcia-Argibay et al. (2019):** 22 studies, 35 effect sizes. **Overall Hedges' g = 0.45** (medium, significant). Anxiety: g = 0.69 (medium-to-large).
- *Psychological Research*, 83, 357-372. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-018-1066-8)

**Ingendoh et al. (2023):** 14 EEG studies on entrainment. **Only 36% supported entrainment** hypothesis. 8 found contradictory results.
- *PLOS ONE*, 18(5), e0286023.

**Perioperative meta-analysis (2025):** 14 trials (N=1,047). Binaural beats significantly reduced perioperative anxiety vs. blank audio. SMD = -1.38, P < 0.0001.

### Null Results
- Lopez-Caballero & Escera (2017): No EEG power changes, no heart rate changes, no skin conductance changes. "Results do not support binaural beats as a potential brainwave entrainment tool."
  - *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience*, 11, 557.
- 1,000 participants (2023): Binaural beats led to **worse** intelligence test scores. *Scientific Reports*.

### Methodological Concerns
- Lack of standardization, inconsistent masking, small samples
- Effects depend on frequency, duration, background noise, individual brain chemistry
- Only 36% of entrainment EEG studies are positive

## 3.5 Transcranial Focused Ultrasound (tFUS) [HARDWARE]

### Capabilities
- Can reach **deep subcortical structures** -- unique among non-invasive methods
- **Millimeter-level precision** targeting
- Regions successfully targeted: amygdala, mPFC, rIFG, motor cortex, somatosensory cortex, thalamus, insula, dACC, frontal eye field

### Behavioral Effects Demonstrated
1. **Anxiety/Fear:** Single session targeting left amygdala reduced BOLD activation; anxiety decreased correlating with amygdala signal reduction.
2. **PTSD:** 15-session protocol: significant decreases in negative affect and PTSD symptoms.
3. **Depression:** Prefrontal targeting: **60% met response criteria; >1/3 achieved remission**.
4. **Choice behavior:** Frontal eye field stimulation created **2:1 to 4:1 choice ratio** biases.
5. **Fear extinction:** Infralimbic cortex stimulation produced lasting improvements.

### Safety
- Pasquinelli et al. (2019): 33 studies reviewed. Adverse effects absent in 30. Only **3% of individuals** had mild/moderate events.
- *Brain Stimulation*, 12(6), 1367-1380.

## 3.7 Subliminal Audio Priming [MACBOOK]

### The Vicary Cinema Hoax (1957) -- DEBUNKED
James Vicary claimed 1/3,000th-second flashes of "Drink Coca-Cola" increased sales 18.1%. In **1962, he admitted fabrication**. The Fort Lee Theater manager denied the experiment ever took place.

### What Science Actually Shows
- **Karremans et al. (2006):** Subliminal brand priming affected choice **only for participants already thirsty**. Must align with existing goal/need.
  - *JESP*, 42(6), 792-798.
- **Kouider et al. (2010):** fMRI showed subliminal speech priming activates Broca's area, Wernicke's area, and insula -- all **without conscious awareness**.
  - *NeuroImage*, 49(1), 922-929.

### Consensus
- Small, short-lived effects on attention, preference, and immediate behavior
- Only works when prime **aligns with existing wants** (goal-dependent)
- Brain responses to subliminal stimuli are real and measurable
- Vicary-style dramatic effects are debunked

## 3.9 fMRI Studies: Unconscious Neural Response to Sub-Threshold Audio

### Key Studies

1. **Weichenberger et al. (2017):** 12 Hz infrasound at near-threshold during resting-state fMRI. Significantly higher connectivity in right superior temporal gyrus and anterior cingulate cortex. *PLoS ONE* 12(4): e0174420.

2. **Brooks et al. (2012):** Meta-analysis -- subliminal stimuli activate **amygdala, hippocampus, anterior cingulate, insular cortex, and primary visual cortex**. Subcortical activation occurs independently of consciousness. *NeuroImage*, 59(3), 2962-2973.

3. **Yang et al. (2014):** Subliminal stimuli specifically linked to increased activation in right fusiform gyrus and right insula. *BMC Psychology*, 2, 52.

4. **Kouider et al. (2010):** Subliminal speech priming activates Broca's and Wernicke's areas without conscious awareness.

### Brain Regions Responsive to Sub-Threshold Audio
- Superior temporal gyrus (auditory cortex)
- Anterior cingulate cortex (salience monitoring)
- Insula (interoception)
- Amygdala (emotion)
- Hippocampus (memory)
- Broca's and Wernicke's areas (language, even subliminal speech)

# 6. ETHICS

## 6.1 Neurorights Frameworks

### Ienca & Andorno (2017) -- Four Neurorights
1. **Cognitive liberty** -- Right to alter one's mental states AND freedom from unauthorized alteration
2. **Mental privacy** -- Protection of neural data
3. **Mental integrity** -- Protection from unauthorized manipulation of neural computation
4. **Psychological continuity** -- Protection of personal identity

Existing human rights frameworks are **insufficient** for neurotechnology.
- *Life Sciences, Society and Policy*, 13, 5.

### Chile's Neurorights Law (2021)
- **First country** to enshrine neurorights in constitutional law
- Amended Article 19(1) of Chilean Constitution
- Protections: mental privacy, personal identity, free will of thought, equitable access, anti-discrimination based on neural data
- Limitation: implementing bill remains stalled
- [UNESCO Courier](https://courier.unesco.org/en/articles/chile-pioneering-protection-neurorights)

### UNESCO Recommendation on Ethics of Neurotechnology (2025)
- Adopted by **190+ countries** in November 2025
- Upholds "inviolability of the human mind"
- Specifically addresses subliminal marketing and "dream manipulation"
- Non-binding but broadly adopted
- [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-neurotechnology)

## 6.2 IRB/Ethics Board Positions

- No specific IRB position statement on frequency-based behavioral research exists
- Falls under general Belmont Report principles (respect for persons, beneficence, justice)
- **Paradox:** Seeking informed consent for neural manipulation while manipulating neural processes necessary for consent
- When minimal risk, IRBs allow modified consent (post-interaction consent, verbal assent)

## 6.3 "Ambient Design" vs. "Covert Manipulation"

### The Ethical Line
- **Nudge theory** (Thaler & Sunstein): "Reflective endorsement test" -- ethical if the person would approve upon reflection
- **Dark patterns** (Brignull, 2010): Now illegal under EU Digital Services Act and CCPA
- **Key distinction:** Would consumers endorse the intervention if they knew about it?
  - Playing pleasant music in a store: passes test
  - Embedding imperceptible frequencies to alter decisions: likely does not

### Where Regulators Draw the Line
- Transparent atmospheric design (music, lighting, scent): **Accepted**
- Subliminal messaging below conscious threshold: **Prohibited in broadcasting**
- AI-driven subliminal manipulation: **Banned by EU AI Act Article 5** (penalties up to EUR 35M or 7% global turnover)

## 6.4 Informed Consent Requirements

- **Research:** Standard IRB approval required. Deception protocols possible but require post-study debriefing.
- **Commercial deployment:** No consent for ambient music. Frequency-specific devices (Mosquito) challenged on human rights grounds but no consent required in most jurisdictions.
- **Military:** Operational deployment (LRAD) does not require consent from targets.

## 6.5 UN/Geneva Conventions on Sonic Weapons

- **CCW (1980):** No specific protocol addresses acoustic weapons
- **Protocol I, Article 35:** Prohibits weapons causing "superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering" -- potentially applicable
- **Article 36:** Requires legal review of ALL new weapons including "non-lethal" -- acoustic devices should fall under this
- **ICRC:** Emphasizes Article 36 reviews must cover acoustic devices. No dedicated policy paper found.
- [Article 36 briefing](https://article36.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/acoustic-weapons.pdf)

## 6.6 Neuroethics of Non-Consensual Neural Influence

- Ienca & Andorno: "mental integrity" right specifically protects against unauthorized neural manipulation
- UNESCO (2025): neurotechnology "should never be used to exert undue influence or manipulation"
- Key concern: **asymmetry of knowledge** -- manipulator understands mechanism while target does not, violating autonomy

# 8. THE FRONTIER

## 8.1 What's Currently Impossible But Theoretically Achievable

### Precision Behavioral Targeting
Current: can shift broad brain states (calm, focus, sleep). Cannot target specific behaviors ("buy this", "walk left").

**Temporal Interference (TI) stimulation** is the most promising bridge. Two high-frequency electrical fields create a low-frequency "beat" deep in the brain. In 2025, Grossman's group showed TI stimulation of hippocampus improved memory in 21 early Alzheimer's patients.
- [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01665-z)

A **2026 Nature study** demonstrated rapid modulation of choice behavior using transcranial focused ultrasound on human frontal eye fields -- one of the first demonstrations of ultrasound changing a specific decision in real time.
- [Nature Communications](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-69854-7)

### Individual Frequency Response Profiling
Each person's alpha peak frequency varies (7-14 Hz) and is highly heritable/stable. Stimulation matched to individual alpha produces faster learning.

A 2026 study found "Conversation Gamma Frequency" (CGF) -- personalized gamma derived from task engagement -- outperformed both fixed 40 Hz and individual gamma frequency for neural synchronization.

**Implication:** Personalized frequency profiles could become as diagnostic as blood type, but cheap rapid profiling doesn't exist at consumer scale yet.

## 8.2 Frequency + AI: Adaptive Systems [MACBOOK for software layer]

### Closed-Loop Neuromodulation

- **Elemind** (MIT spinoff): Consumer headband reads EEG, delivers phase-locked pink noise via bone conduction to suppress alpha and accelerate sleep. 76% fell asleep faster, average 48% improvement.
- **Neuromorphic neuromodulation (2025):** Framework for low-power edge chips running on-device neural net training -- personalized closed-loop without cloud.
- **Sleep closed-loop (2025):** EEG wearable targeting slow oscillations during sleep with precisely-timed acoustic stimulation.
  - [BioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.09.642252v1.full)

### Machine Learning + EEG
- Deep neural networks leverage multimodal data (fMRI, DTI, EEG) to predict individual responses before stimulation
- EEG-triggered TMS delivers pulses at specific phases of brain oscillations
- AI-driven multimodal brain-state decoding for personalized TENS validated for chronic pain

## 8.3 Swarm-Level Coordination

- **Concert brainwave synchronization:** EEG at live performances shows audience brain waves synchronize; degree correlates with enjoyment.
- **Duet pianists:** 20 pairs showed brainwave coupling at performance tempo.
- **Heartbeat synchronization:** Concert heartbeat sync predicts enjoyment/emotional response.
- **Optimal entrainment:** Brain entrainment to electronic music peaks at **1.65 Hz (~99 BPM)**.
- **Implication:** Shared acoustic environments could theoretically be engineered for group synchronization -- largely unexplored commercially.

## 8.4 Brain-Computer Interfaces Using Frequency

### SSVEP BCIs (Visual)
- Most mature frequency-input BCI paradigm
- 2025: Lightweight deep learning (GZSL-Lite: 85% accuracy, 4,270 parameters, 0.47 ms inference) enabling mobile SSVEP-BCIs

### Auditory BCIs [MACBOOK-relevant]
- ASSR-based: modulated tones at different frequencies (32 Hz, 38 Hz) to each ear
- 2025: Spatial auditory BCI achieved 80.45% online accuracy at 12.04 bits/min
- 2025: EEG-based auditory BCI communication in **completely locked-in patient**

### Ultrasound Neural Interfaces [HARDWARE]
- **Forest Neurotech:** Device smaller than key fob, images >30% of brain volume. Partnership with Butterfly Network.
- **Aleph Neuro (2026):** First 3D ultrasound brain vasculature image through intact skull. Resolution 100x greater than CT.
- **Sonogenetics (Salk Institute):** Ultrasound + gene therapy. TRPA1 protein makes neurons ultrasound-sensitive. **ARPA-H awarded $41.3M**.

## 8.5 The Path Forward

**Current (2026):**
- Crude: binaural beats, background music tempo, white noise
- Emerging: closed-loop acoustic sleep (Elemind), 40 Hz gamma (Cognito), personalized frequency matching
- Research-grade: TI stimulation, focused ultrasound, sonogenetics

**Near-Term (2027-2030):**
- Individual frequency profiling via consumer EEG + AI
- Closed-loop expands to focus, mood, anxiety
- Ultrasound devices shrink to consumer wearable
- Acoustic metamaterials enable deep brain targeting without surgery

**Medium-Term (2030-2035):**
- Sonogenetics enters clinical trials
- AI predicts individual responses from 5-minute EEG baseline
- Group entrainment becomes engineering discipline

**Long-Term (2035+):**
- Precision behavioral targeting as non-invasive spatial resolution reaches sub-millimeter
- "Frequency prescriptions" prescribed like medications
- Ethical/regulatory frameworks lag technology by 5-10 years
